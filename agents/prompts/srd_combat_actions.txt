COMBAT ACTIONS (SRD)

YOUR TURN. On your turn you can move a distance up to your speed and take one
action. You decide whether to move first or act first. If you want to do
something the list does not offer, end your turn instead; the engine decides
what is possible, not you.

MOVEMENT AND POSITION. You can break up your movement, moving some before and
some after your action. Every foot of movement in difficult terrain costs one
extra foot; that doubling applies even if more than one thing in a square
makes it difficult. Dropping prone costs nothing. Standing up costs an amount
of movement equal to half your speed. Crawling one foot in difficult terrain
costs three feet of movement. You can move through a non-hostile creature's
space, and you can move through a hostile creature's space only if that
creature is at least two sizes larger or smaller than you; either way, another
creature's space is difficult terrain for you. You cannot stop in a space
occupied by another creature.

ACTION. When you take your action you can do one of the following.

Attack. You make one melee or ranged attack. Some features, such as Extra
Attack, let one Attack action make more than one attack.

Cast a Spell. You cast a spell with a casting time of one action. A spell with
a casting time of one bonus action is cast with your bonus action instead, and
if you cast a spell with a bonus action you cannot cast another spell that turn
except a cantrip with a casting time of one action.

Dash. You gain extra movement for the current turn equal to your speed, after
any modifiers are applied. With a speed of 30 feet, Dashing gives you 60 feet
of movement on that turn.

Disengage. Your movement does not provoke opportunity attacks for the rest of
the turn.

Dodge. Until the start of your next turn, any attack roll made against you has
disadvantage if you can see the attacker, and you make Dexterity saving throws
with advantage. You lose this benefit if you are incapacitated or if your speed
drops to 0.

Help. You lend your aid to another creature in the completion of a task: the
creature gains advantage on the next ability check it makes to perform that
task, provided it makes the check before the start of your next turn.
Alternatively you can aid a friendly creature in attacking a creature within 5
feet of you; the first attack roll that creature makes against the target
before the start of your next turn has advantage.

Hide. You make a Dexterity (Stealth) check in an attempt to hide. If you
succeed you gain certain benefits: you are an unseen attacker, and attack rolls
against you have disadvantage while you remain hidden. You give away your
position the moment you make an attack or cast a spell with a verbal component.

Ready. You choose a perceivable circumstance and an action you will take in
response to it, holding your action until that trigger occurs. Readying a spell
requires spending the slot when you ready it and holding concentration on it.

Search. You devote your attention to finding something, usually with a Wisdom
(Perception) or an Intelligence (Investigation) check.

Use an Object. You interact with a second object — you get one free object
interaction each turn, so this action is for the second one — or you use an
object that requires an action, such as drinking a potion of healing.

BONUS ACTIONS. You take a bonus action only when a feature, spell or ability
says you can, and you get one at most on a turn. You choose when to take it
during your turn unless the bonus action's timing is specified. You cannot take
a bonus action while incapacitated.

REACTIONS. A reaction is an instant response to a trigger, and you can take
only one per round. When you take a reaction you cannot take another until the
start of your next turn. The most common reaction is the opportunity attack:
when a hostile creature that you can see moves out of your reach, you can use
your reaction to make one melee attack against it. The attack happens right
before the creature leaves your reach. A creature that Disengages, teleports,
or is moved by something other than its own movement does not provoke.

MAKING AN ATTACK. Choose a target within range, apply any modifiers to the
attack roll, and compare the total to the target's Armor Class. Rolling a 20
on the d20 is a critical hit and always hits regardless of modifiers; rolling a
1 always misses. On a critical hit you roll all of the attack's damage dice
twice and add them together, then add your modifiers as normal.

RANGED ATTACKS. A ranged weapon has a normal range and a long range in
parentheses. Attacking a target beyond normal range imposes disadvantage; you
cannot attack a target beyond long range. You have disadvantage on a ranged
attack roll when a hostile creature that can see you and is not incapacitated
is within 5 feet of you.

MELEE ATTACKS, GRAPPLING AND SHOVING. A melee attack typically reaches 5 feet.
Instead of making an attack you can use one attack to grapple (an Athletics
check contested by the target's Athletics or Acrobatics; on a success the
target is grappled) or to shove (the same contest; on a success you either
knock the target prone or push it 5 feet away). You can grapple or shove only a
creature no more than one size larger than you.

TWO-WEAPON FIGHTING. When you take the Attack action and attack with a light
melee weapon in one hand, you can use a bonus action to attack with a different
light melee weapon in your other hand. You do not add your ability modifier to
the damage of that bonus attack unless the modifier is negative.

UNSEEN ATTACKERS AND TARGETS. When you attack a target you cannot see, you have
disadvantage on the attack roll, whether or not you are guessing correctly.
When a creature cannot see you, you have advantage on attack rolls against it.

COVER. A target with half cover has a +2 bonus to AC and Dexterity saving
throws; three-quarters cover gives +5. A target with total cover cannot be
targeted directly by an attack or a spell, although some area effects may still
reach it. Only the most protective degree of cover applies.

CONCENTRATION. Some spells require concentration to keep their magic active.
You lose concentration if you cast another spell that requires concentration,
if you are incapacitated or killed, or if you take damage and fail a
Constitution saving throw whose DC equals 10 or half the damage taken,
whichever number is higher.
