DAMAGE, HEALING AND DYING (SRD)

HIT POINTS. Damage subtracts from hit points, and any damage in excess of what
reduces you to 0 hit points is simply lost, except in the case of massive
damage below. Hit points regained from healing never exceed your hit point
maximum, and healing an unconscious creature above 0 hit points wakes it.

TEMPORARY HIT POINTS. Temporary hit points are a buffer, not healing: damage
reduces them first, and losing them does not reduce your real hit points.
Temporary hit points do not stack — when you receive some while you still have
others, you choose which to keep. They are lost when you finish a long rest.

DROPPING TO 0 HIT POINTS. When you drop to 0 hit points you either die outright
or fall unconscious. You fall unconscious and are dying, unless the damage that
took you down is massive damage: if the leftover damage after you reach 0 equals
or exceeds your hit point maximum, you die instantly.

DEATH SAVING THROWS. When you start your turn at 0 hit points you make a death
saving throw: roll a d20 with no modifier, and 10 or higher is a success. On
your third success you become stable; on your third failure you die. The
successes and failures do not need to be consecutive, and both are reset to
zero when you regain any hit points or become stable. Rolling a 1 on the d20
counts as two failures, and rolling a 20 means you regain 1 hit point.

DAMAGE AT 0 HIT POINTS. If you take any damage while you are at 0 hit points,
you suffer a death saving throw failure. If the damage is from a critical hit
you suffer two failures instead. If the damage equals or exceeds your hit point
maximum you die outright.

STABILIZING. A stable creature does not make death saving throws, though it
remains at 0 hit points and unconscious. It regains 1 hit point after 1d4 hours
unless it takes damage first; taking damage makes it dying again. You can use
your action to administer first aid to an unconscious creature and attempt to
stabilize it, which requires a successful DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check.

MONSTERS AND DEATH. Most monsters simply die at 0 hit points rather than
falling unconscious and making death saving throws.

KNOCKING A CREATURE OUT. When you reduce a creature to 0 hit points with a
melee attack, you can choose to knock it out instead: it falls unconscious and
is stable.

RESISTANCE, VULNERABILITY AND IMMUNITY. Resistance halves the damage of a given
type, vulnerability doubles it, and immunity reduces it to nothing. Resistance
and then vulnerability are applied after all other modifiers, and multiple
instances of either count only once.
